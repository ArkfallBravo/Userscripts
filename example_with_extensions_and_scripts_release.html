My good friend Cart/Vvvase said this effortlessly swept every Type O album, and while I don't really hear the comparison beyond the obvious Ethereal Wave influence, this is an incredible album. If anything, it feels akin to Koi No Yokan but leaning far more into the already incredibly dreamlike atmosphere. Aleah's vocals remind me a lot of Chelsea Wolfe, but dare I say stronger. It's strangely soft and lush for Doom, but instead of making the riffage fall flat, it makes it float ever higher and lulls me into a quiet euphoria. My only real complaints are that it runs a little long in the tooth near the end, and while I do really like the softer tracks like the title song, I tend to prefer it when the band blends doomy distortion with angelic beauty (luckily that's the majority of the LP). Really tragic what happened to Aleah. On one hand, I would've loved to have heard more from this group on future LPs, but considering how much the sound and image of the band was indebted to her, it's probably for the best that this was a one and done project.

An Ode to Melancholy.
To know the history of Hour of the Nightingale, the posthumous album by Trees of Eternity, is to mourn in the company of its tearful harmonies. It is to surrender to the rhythm of its dragging cadence. It is, ultimately, to give oneself, prostrate, to the delicate disconsolation that dictates its tone from the first to the final chord.
Aleah Stanbridge and Juha Raivio (Swallow the Sun) shared a life together. They were further kindred spirits in musical projects. And, as a result of this symbiosis, they brought to fruition perhaps one of the most elegant sculptures of introspective music: Hour of the Nightingale. A true ode to melancholy.
Hour of the Nightingale was recorded in the distant year of 2014. Recondite in a silent hiatus, it only sparked into life in November 2016, gestated already after the sorrowful passing of Aleah Stanbridge. The vocalist, lyricist, and composer succumbed after facing a battle against cancer. She departed prematurely from the stage of life, which closed its curtains upon her on April 18, 2016, at the age of 39. She remains, however, ever since, alive in memory and eternalized through the paths of the indissoluble intangible heritage she bequeathed: her compositions and her voice, of distinct aesthetic beauty.
The creative union of Aleah Stanbridge and Juha Raivio found, in Hour of the Nightingale, its maximum and most potent artistic expression. The sorumbatic inspiration of its concept, both musical and lyrical, amalgamates with the very funereal climate lamentably prophesied: Aleah’s death. Regardless, the passing of the vocalist, sorrowful under any light, bestowed upon Hour of the Nightingale a particularly intimate, and even spiritual, dimension of elevated and sensory homage.
These inaugural considerations may perhaps already synthesize, in a self-sufficient manner, the general spirit that guides the musicality of Hour of the Nightingale. As for the rest, it falls to the listener the task of immersion in their own particular journey. Nevertheless, in respect to the stature by which Hour of the Nightingale masterfully rises, there is room for a more detailed deepening.
To define is to limit. To carve musical diameters into predefined labels is to narrow the notion of its fathomless frontiers. Merely for a superficial understanding, however, Trees of Eternity draws its sap from the most melancholic fountains of doom metal, under the beat of tempos ranging from moderate to slow, with various rhythmic breaks.
The vocals, performed almost entirely by Aleah Stanbridge, confer a gothic temperament to the musicality of Hour of the Nightingale. A contrast between the beautiful and the bleak, which, in musical mysticism, do not repel each other but coexist harmoniously. A confrontation between the heavy, burdened instrumental density on one pole, and the markedly clear, crystalline, and angelic contours of the vocal lines as a counterpoint.
This emotional and atmospheric ambience is further intensified by the mournful twin-guitar arrangements, so present throughout the listen, and by the climatic musical walls masterfully executed by the keyboard work. My Requiem, the opening track, illustrates this amalgam of multi-layered sentiments. It further mirrors the pattern of virtues that runs through this entire artistic-creative pilgrimage.
Hour of the Nightingale reaches, however, the pinnacle of its sonic apotheosis with the precise violin incursions. Sprinkling the listen, these insertions confer upon its general spirit an intimate, romantic-melancholic vein. My Requiem, already cited, and A Million Tears reflect, by way of example, these qualities. It should be noted, in passing, that the violin arrangements were performed with a measured balance, without excess or exhibitionism, thus preserving a perfect equilibrium among all the instrumental resources gathered within the compositions. The use of violins as a bridge to melancholic elevation mirrors a common musical device in the genre, but excesses can weary the listener. Some ensembles made lavish use of it and achieved notably competent results, such as My Dying Bride and The Sins of Thy Beloved. In this regard, Trees of Eternity wagered on conciseness. And they were unerring. They molded, with parsimony, the role of each instrument, allowing, as a result, a sensory experience as fertile as it is balanced.
In a panoramic view, this is the frame and the painting upon which Hour of the Nightingale is composed.
Should this review still afford space for additions, beyond the general verve already embroidered, it is worth highlighting the particularly ghostly atmosphere of Eye of Night, as well as the feeling of a sweet oceanic sailing experienced in the acoustic ambience of Sinking Ships.
Finally, enriching its fertility even further, Hour of the Nightingale features special guest appearances. Highly special, indeed. Mick Moss, from Antimatter, lent his clear and powerful voice to Condemned to Silence, while Nick Holmes, vocalist of the iconic Paradise Lost, graced the listen with his somber and unmistakable timbres in Gallows Bird.
Cataloging the genre of Trees of Eternity is an arduous and, perhaps, unnecessary task. The ensemble gathers elements of Gothic music and Doom Metal, but with a strong identity. Regardless of the labels one might wish to employ, the group achieved the principal goal: it transmitted sensoriality. It touched the listener through the thread of the musical code, transporting them to dreamlike parallels - at times celestial, at times lugubrious - and to sorumbatic states of mind of contemplation, meditation, and introspection. A core that, stylistically, perhaps inhabits the common layers of intersecting circles of doom metal, gothic metal, doomgaze, and atmospheric music in general.
Lamentably, with the untimely departure of one of the ensemble's members and mentors, Trees of Eternity released no further unreleased material. It unquestionably possessed the potential for a long-lasting career with consistent quality. By irony of fate and those bitter unforeseen events of life, these ideas were buried and interred even before their conception. They will never take shape or see the light of day. A pity.
In any case, and even because of this insolate context of desolate farewells and losses, Hour of the Nightingale will surely function as a kind of cult material. Most importantly, however: it will eternalize a just tribute to the memory of an artist who closed the curtains on the stage of life so prematurely, but who bequeathed an unerasable heritage: music, that mystical intangible component that always finds its way to the heart. With this sentence, I ended the review I wrote, at another time, about Aleah's solo work. I thus end, now as well, this analysis of Hour of the Nightingale, with the same tribute and with a feeling of deep respect and admiration for this posthumous work.
Enjoy the immersion.

In 2009 as Finnish death-doomers Swallow The Sun worked on acclaimed fourth album New Moon, guitarist Juha Raivio became taken with session backing vocalist Aleah Stanbridge. The two developed a romantic relationship and Raivio became convinced that Stanbridge belonged up front. With STS drummer Kai Hahto on board along with death-doom legends October Tide (Norrman brothers Fredrick on lead and Mattias on bass) a supergroup was born.
And was sadly not long for this world. Recording sessions begun in 2014 were interrupted when Stanbridge was diagnosed with cancer. She survived to complete the album but died before its release and what should have been the start of a career was its end. Her loss profoundly affected Raivio and both of his next two releases (When A Shadow Is Forced Into The Light and Aleah) pay tribute to her.
So HOTN stands as Trees Of Eternity’s only work. It’s a real marmite album - if you want slow, light, morbid gothy death-doom with female vocals, you are likely going to love this. If you don’t want that you may find it an interminable slog, with little variety in tempo and mood across its hour long runtime. Yet there is some undeniably moving material such as My Requiem where Stanbridge’s intentionally icy monotone ethereally sings of her own imminent death and her partner’s grief:

“My season’s come to an end
Too late you’re calling out my name
To raise me up out of my grave
Alive in your memory I’ll stay
If you shun these waters where I lay”

In combination with gothic strings, Raivio’s crashing rhythm work and Fredrik’s sinister but strangely beautiful ringing guitar, My Requiem is a rare and poignant work of self-mourning. It’s not alone; see also the title track where Fredrik uses a spaghetti western tone to contribute to the echoing nature of the music as Stanbridge sings words of comfort to those she leaves behind:

“Saline waters won’t raise the dead
Grief will haunt those who forget that night is not the end
Embrace this as a nightingale in song
In the darkest hour of shadows she belongs
Her voice reflects the colours of the dawn”

Its slow, stark nature boldly confronts the grief yet to come and halfway through when Raivio and Hahto crash in to transition the song from dark folk to doom metal it’s a powerful moment. October Tide fans will find music much lighter than they are used to but the dynamic variance works. The excellent Eye Of Night begins as Fredrik’s spooky guitar chiming beneath Stanbridge’s ghostly voice before Raivio and Hahto bring in a heavy groove followed by a smooth transition to an unexpectedly catchy chorus. The seven minute super doom of A Million Tears would make My Dying Bride tell Trees Of Eternity to cheer the fuck up. Stanbridge’s tear-streaked voice trembles through the delicate verses before a big sorrowful doom chorus where her voice rises, calling to you from deep within the forest, drawing you further into the trees.
By now it should be pretty clear that if this is the sort of thing you like, then this is the sort of thing you'll like. No play-acting or mall-gothery here - it’s a creative work made in the knowledge of the greatest loss there is and listened to in its aftermath. People seeking fun have already hit the exits, but for those interested in how music can be a vehicle for mourning and wallowing in grief as well as finding solace and comfort, THOTN is recommended.

This is a brand of doom that feels significantly lighter than what I'm usually familiar with. The instrumentation isn't aggressive at all, if anything, most of it feels suspended and ethereal. Nonetheless, the band still manages to make you feel anguish and desperation at the right moments. If more "classic" doom is all about the fear of dying and mourning over your past, "Hour of the Nightingale" feels like it's about someone who's already dead, whose spirit is looking for peace. For most of the time, the atmosphere is less oppressing and more ghastly in nature, thanks to the importance that was given to clean guitar sections, accompanied by strings. This also makes the heavier parts stand out more and feel more rewarding, as they're in contrast with their more mellow counterparts. The songs all progress flawlessly, and I was hardly ever bored: this album is really a little doom gem.

Trees of Eternity це гурт, що був сформований навколо "скандинавської Doom Metal тусовки", найбільш відомими представниками якої, напевно, є брати Norrman з Katatonia та October Tide.

Але в першу чергу, я, звісно, маю на увазі гітариста Swallow the Sun - Juha Raivio.

Через це, деякі Gothic Doom елементи, на цьому альбомі, дуже схожі на ті, що ми вже чули в його "основному" гурті.

Проте "схожі" - не значить однакові, і виступають вони тут, швидше, "візитівкою", чи приємною "великодкою".

Та і взагалі, Juha тут далеко не "головний герой", адже увесь шарм альбому створюється навколо чудового, "мрійливого" вокалу Aleah, яка вже встигла попрацювати з Juha, будучи "гостьовою" вокалісткою для пісні Lights on the Lake.

Aleah Stanbridge - є шведською виконавицею, що працювала у таких жанрах як Dream Pop та Ethereal Wave , та взагалі не була "метал-співачкою".
І це, на мою думку, пішло гурту тільки на користь, адже замість типового для готік металу, виразного та яскравого,вокалу - ми чуємо "легкий, повітряний", та транквільний" спів.

ЇЇ вокал відчувається настільки тендітним та "крихким", що часом переймаєшся, як би "металева гострота", випадково, не зіпсувала це відчуття.

На щастя, між Juha та Aleah відбулась "справжня хімія" і результат їх сумісної праці вийшов, напрочуд, органічним та ідеально збалансованим симбіозом "етерної" атмосфери, та насиченого "думово-гототичного" звучання.

Але, нажаль, цей цікавий "эксперимент", став першим і останнім альбомом, шо випустив цей гурт...

В 2016 році, під час пост-продакшену альбому, Aleah померла від раку.

І ця сумна подія - поклала гурту край, а лейбл AMF Music - не бачили сенс випускати вже, фактично, готовий альбом.

Проте, на допомогу прийшов інді-лейбл - Svart Records. Вони розуміли усю велич та важливість цієї унікальної роботи, та, із гордістю, подарували їй життя.

Альбом Hour of the Nightingale важко назвати черговим "гарним метал релізом".

Адже перед нами - справжній витвір мистецтва, який Juha Raivio називав:
"Найважливішою роботою усього його життя"
Та в якому, одночасно, знаходять собі місце не тільки - морок, загадковість та містичність, але і відчуття, просто неперевершеної, краси.

En memoria de Aleah
Descubrí este disco indagando un poco acerca de las colaboraciones y side projects de los miembros de Swallow the sun y para mi gran agrado vi que en esta banda se encontraba Aleah Starbridge que tiene muchísimas colaboraciones en el mundo del metal con grandes bandas y al ser su proyecto principal me anime a darle la oportunidad a su único trabajo discográfico. Al adentrarme ya en el primer track My Requiem supe desde ese instante que me encontraba frente a una obra maestra con todas las letras y no me equivoque cuando continúe escuchando el resto tracks, todos son de una calidad muy alta, destaco la voz casi fantasmal y pasional de Aleah que en cada canción nos regala esa carica al oído lleno de un sentimiento muy personal que solo ella puede contarte al oído mientras que el resto de instrumentos a cargo de grandes músicos del Doom Metal pasando por el arte de la portada a cargo de la mente detrás de Les Discrets terminan de confirmar lo que para mi es una obra de arte no solo del metal sino de la música en general una experiencia que solo Aleah, una amante de la música y del metal, te pueden regalar como su ultimo Réquiem
